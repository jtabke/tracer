# Studio task and interaction contract

Status: proposed product/UX contract for design review, October 13, 2026. Project studio is the selected direction; this contract, its compositions and the tasks below are **not implementation approval**. No production code is changed here. The author/connector/reviewer roles are working hypotheses, not researched personas. Fixture records and observations are fictional.

This contract extends the [design study](README.md) beyond one prepared investigation. The [independent product gate](../team-review/product-design-gate.md) remains open. Its purpose is to define a few complete user tasks and the states needed to judge their design before optimizing controls.

## Product outcome and organizing idea

An engineer can express one part of a design, connect it to existing work, and explain what a subsequent change means for its recorded evidence without repeatedly reconstructing context. This serves UN-001–005, PN-01–09, owner needs UR-1–2/4/7/13/15–23 and the candidate ND/SC journeys in [user needs](../../user-needs.md) and [user journeys](../../user-journeys.md). None establishes a universal mandatory hierarchy, automatic impact policy or regulated approval workflow.

**A scope is the work; a representation is how it is viewed; a selection is the object currently inspected.** Scope origin, selected relationship/direction or trace recipe, filters, loaded bounds and revision context are distinct. Selecting a neighbor does not change origin. Making that neighbor the new scope is explicit and has a return to the previous scope. Graph, Table, Reading, relationship columns and Matrix/Coverage operate around this model rather than becoming duplicate global destinations.

One current item, link, captured input or historical revision occupies the primary contextual surface at a time. Editing replaces the relevant read content. A full captured comparison can replace the canvas temporarily and name its return scope; it must not stack a second copy of the current editor beneath itself. One identity may appear in several paths, but has one authoritative draft.

## Small complete tasks to demonstrate

### A. Aster: author one allocation and connect an existing shared requirement

**Task:** finish a small Stop-input allocation while seeing its sibling release requirement. Finished means saved content and explicit trace are explainable; it does not mean verified or clinically acceptable.

Use an explicitly labeled **new-design rehearsal copy**, not the untouched full seed: open SYS-07, retain its existing CMP-05 allocation, and make CMP-06 not yet authored in this rehearsal. The full [medical seed](../../../examples/medical-device-example.ts) already contains all three and their links. Do not portray the full seed as missing them.

1. From Project/library/search, open SYS-07, “Prioritize Stop input,” in its outgoing `decomposes_into` scope. Show the complete supervisor statement and existing shared CMP-05, “Release valve flow.” The scope names what its siblings mean; it is not an inferred tree.
2. Start **Create in this scope**. The draft exposes `component_requirement`, the proposed CMP-06 key/title and full statement: “A stable Stop-switch assertion shall be delivered to the supervisor within 100 ms without multiple-trigger side effects.” Keep SYS-07's statement available as reference without another editor.
3. Review the proposed relationship separately from the new content: SYS-07 → `decomposes_into` → CMP-06, with a human scope description. Offer explicit **Create and connect** or save unlinked. The action must not quietly choose a parent or add a second relationship.
4. Return to the working scope with the new identity selected, its saved revision and the actual relationship visible. The two sibling requirements remain readable together. A fresh read/reload of a native implementation must show the saved identity and link; a local prototype must call these preview results.
5. Inspect CMP-05's other recorded allocations: SYS-06 and SYS-08 also decompose into this same requirement. Follow OUT-04 and VER-06 as existing context. This explains shared identity, not replicated release valves or successful verification. RUN-06 records a simulated failed passive-release result.

**Acceptance challenge:** can someone explain the newly authored obligation, why it is connected, and why the already shared CMP-05 was reused? Can they cancel without changing data, and recover from a conflict without losing their proposed statement? A created node and a success toast alone do not complete the task.

### B. Gatherline: define a missing test without claiming implementation or execution

**Task:** add an acceptance scenario for redacted diagnostics and connect the obligation and descriptive implementation allocation. Finished means test definition and trace exist; it does not mean code was imported or a test ran.

Use actual STORY-12, WORK-10 and EVT-02 from the [technology seed](../../../examples/technology-example.ts). STORY-12 → `implemented_by` → WORK-10 already exists. WORK-10 → `changes` → COMP-03/COMP-08 records descriptive work allocation. STORY-12 and WORK-10 have no `checked_by` scenario in this seed. Their artifact paths are invented descriptions, not imported executable code.

1. Open STORY-12's scope and read its diagnostics obligation together with WORK-10 and the relevant event contract. Display “No recorded test scenario in this checked scope” only after a complete compatible-link read. Do not label the implementation complete from its link.
2. Create TEST-PREVIEW-11 as **newly authored study content**, with a proposed diagnostic-redaction scenario drawn from the existing obligation. This key/scenario is not in the seed. Show test definition as the object being created; no run/outcome field implies execution.
3. Explicitly connect STORY-12 → `checked_by` → TEST-PREVIEW-11 and WORK-10 → `checked_by` → TEST-PREVIEW-11. The story supplies acceptance intent; the descriptive work record identifies what is being checked. Show each relationship's extent and source-version annotation.
4. Return to the same scope. The recorded test-definition gap is addressed; execution evidence remains absent. The test identity is shared across both connections. An “implemented” or “verified” badge is not a valid finished state.

This is the lighter counterpart to task A: project vocabulary changes, while authoring, contextual connection, shared identity and result boundaries remain consistent. No regulated sign-off is inserted.

Receipts are created **before** the declared changed record is updated. These are actual persisted revision mismatches when seeded, not freshness strings painted onto unchanged data.

Interaction sequence for each case:

1. Open a named scope or locate the changed record. Read its current content and actual upstream/downstream connections. Multilevel Graph and aligned reading retain siblings and shared objects; direct criteria-to-test links cannot substitute for the design/implementation path.
2. Select the recorded result, then its exact captured-input reference. A receipt edge is provenance, distinct from an ordinary native relationship. The comparison names the result, captured input, captured revision, current revision and configuration when present.
3. Read actual changed declared fields first. Show missing/unknown inputs prominently; compact matching inputs remain reachable. Summarize the whole input set's changed/unknown/matching states so opening one input never hides other consequential differences. An unavailable current record remains unknown rather than an invented empty statement.
4. Inspect the exact historical input when needed and return to its comparison. Inspect current content explicitly; it does not silently replace the captured object. Configuration membership names its exact immutable pins, without claiming a complete historical graph or production BOM.
5. Review the **captured result against the disclosed current comparison**, with a decision and rationale. Retain observation, comparison and relevant inputs while judging. One immutable review concerns this disclosed scope; it is not approval of the individual graph node or the whole product. Relevant new drift requires deliberate adoption, not a silent token refresh.
6. Return to the originating scope with selection and unsaved unrelated content intact. The historical outcome stays unchanged. Current drift, recorded judgment and whether that judgment still applies remain separate facts. Potential next actions may be user-authored records/connections; do not promise a new automatic impact or reassessment engine.

The realistic link schema requires `scope`, `source_version` and `review`. They are **project-owned descriptive fields**. `source_version` is not a substitute for the engine's write preconditions, and a `review` string is not enforced approval. A proposed link editor must preserve required values and make their meaning understandable rather than treating the arrow alone as the complete record.

## Navigation, hierarchy and visual-versus-copy decisions

Project presents ways to resume or begin recognizable work. Work provides library/search/saved-scope entry and one active scope. Evaluations and Configurations may be dedicated catalogues of different immutable records, but must open the same canonical inspection owner when reached from a trace. Model explains project definitions, separate from actual recorded product evidence. Exact route count/names remain a composition choice.

There is **one representation toolbar**, local to the displayed working surface. Graph/Table are not repeated in the primary rail. Reading, relationship columns and Matrix/Coverage remain visible when space permits, with accessible toolbar overflow for genuinely constrained width rather than a blanket “Advanced” accordion. Scope relationship/direction and trace depth appear only when they govern the visible representation. During a focused capture/history/editor task, unrelated graph controls yield space to that task's actions.

| Represent visually                                                           | Keep in precise copy                                                            | Avoid competing authority                                                                                                   |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Branches, shared identities, incoming/outgoing links, feedback and selection | Exact relationship meaning, scope origin, loaded bounds and canonical direction | Do not turn spatial position into mandatory hierarchy or collapse a full trace to an unexplained shortest path.             |
| Sibling alignment and relationship columns                                   | Full obligations, assumptions and connection extent                             | Do not repeat the selected full statement in both Table/Reading and a second inspector body.                                |
| Captured/current columns and changed-field emphasis                          | Actual values, observation, comparison scope, rationale                         | Do not replace detailed changes with a drift badge or use outcome to imply applicability.                                   |
| Pinned/current membership and distinct capture/history treatment             | Capture identity, exact selected revisions and limits                           | Do not mutate the appearance of a recorded build when current content changes.                                              |
| Type workflow with visible criteria/design inputs and explicit loops         | Type/rule definitions and native mapping on selection                           | Do not reverse native arrows to create a pretty process, invent execution, or equate permission with a recorded connection. |

Use icons and color as reinforcement with labels, not the only carriers of type/state/direction. Raw IDs, provenance and rare technical configuration belong to purposeful secondary detail. Full statements are the work, not clutter to summarize away. Captured/current comparison and changed/unknown input access are frequent review needs and must not disappear behind generic collapsibles.

For narrow screens, selection must become a deliberate readable stage with a visible return to the retained scope, not merely content below a long graph. Desktop must demonstrate both simultaneous context/inspection and long comparison reading. The precise docked/focused treatment remains under review; do not shrink typography to preserve a chosen panel arrangement.

## Model journey required alongside the item tasks

Review the full project vocabulary, not a four-type starter stand-in under another project's identity. Show several authored inputs/outputs and at least one explicit feedback route. Requirements supply criteria and design/implementation supplies what is evaluated. A diagram position is not a lifecycle gate.

Configure is one explicit draft with Visual and Text representations. Demonstrate adding or altering a type/connection, selecting exact endpoint constraints, switching modes with the draft intact, invalid text, and save/cancel/conflict. Show one change summary, not duplicate editable definitions. Authored workflow metadata is supported by the native schema; no execution, automatic approval, persisted canvas layout or regulated certification is inferred. The study must label any configuration capability it only illustrates.

## Acceptance boundary before native UI implementation

The next reviewable artifact must demonstrate these **complete small tasks**, their transitions and one important recovery path, rather than every feature at once. It can use explicit rehearsal state and local preview writes, but must state their limitations.

Ask reviewers to explain: what scope is retained, which object is editable, why a shared identity is shared, what was saved, what the old result used, what remains unknown and where work continues. Inspect actual desktop/narrow states. Owner review chooses the consequential composition; independent AI critique may reject it. Browser QA proves specified preview behavior, not native persistence or human usefulness.

Do not count a green test suite, reviewer agreement, a polished initial screen or a prepared route walkthrough as acceptance of the whole application. If an anchor task needs a different composition, revise the concept before optimizing the components.
